## 2. THE THREE CELLS, AND THE THING THAT IS ACTUALLY GOING ON

| | classes C | px | train imgs/class | scalar | layerwise | **GRAN = lay − scal** | **Q = scal / lay** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CIFAR-10 | 10 | 32 | 5000 | 87.879 | 90.893 | **+3.014** | **0.9668** |
| CIFAR-100 | 100 | 32 | 500 | 22.776 | 69.302 | **+46.526** | **0.3286** |
| Tiny-ImageNet | 200 | 64 | 500 | 9.859 | 50.835 | **+40.976** | **0.1939** |

**GRAN in pp is not monotone in C** (+3.0 → +46.5 → +41.0), and that non-monotonicity is an
artefact of the pp scale, not a fact about the phenomenon: a pp gap is arithmetically capped by
the layerwise arm's own accuracy, which falls as the task gets harder. **Q is monotone and is
the right scale.** Registering Q as primary *now*, before the data, is the whole point —
switching scales after seeing a result is the failure CORRECTIONS 117.11 is about.

### 2.1 The mechanism is UNDERFITTING, and this is visible on the train side

The decisive observation, and the reason this is a law rather than a curve fit:

| | scalar train | layerwise train | scalar test | layerwise test |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CIFAR-10 (`final_train`, archive) | 93.8 | 99.7 | 87.9 | 90.9 |
| CIFAR-100 (`final_train`, archive) | **22.8** | 99.0 | 22.8 | 69.3 |
| Tiny-ImageNet (train5, tn1 logs) | **9.76** | 88.7 | 9.86 | 50.8 |

At C ≥ 100 the scalar arm **cannot fit its own training set**. Train ≈ test to within a
percentage point. This is not a generalisation failure and it is not overfitting; it is an
optimisation failure. The layerwise arm on the identical data reaches 99.0 / 88.7 train. The
scalar arm is not at chance either (Tiny-ImageNet chance is 0.5%, the scalar arm sits at 9.9%,
~20× chance) — it climbs for ~12 epochs and then stalls for the remaining ~87.

So the law is not "granularity generalises better at high C". It is: **a single scalar
step size loses the ability to optimise the network at all as class count grows, and per-tensor
step sizes do not.**

### 2.2 Class count, not resolution

The two transitions separate the candidate drivers as far as this corpus can:

* **10 → 100 classes at FIXED 32px, fixed architecture family, fixed batch, fixed everything
  in §1.1's predicate.** Q collapses 0.967 → 0.329. GRAN goes +3.0 → +46.5.
* **100 → 200 classes AND 32px → 64px.** Q continues to fall, 0.329 → 0.194. The resolution
  doubling **adds nothing**; GRAN in pp actually goes *down* (+46.5 → +41.0), entirely
  accounted for by the layerwise ceiling falling from 69.3 to 50.8.

The strongest form of this argument is quantitative, not verbal: **a single exponent in C
alone reproduces both transitions.** Fitting `Q(C) = Q₁₀·(C/10)^(−b)` to each transition
separately gives b = 0.4686 (from the 10→100 step, resolution held fixed) and b = 0.5363 (from
the 100→200 step, which *also* doubles resolution). Those two exponents agree to within 13%.
If resolution carried material weight, the second transition — the one that changes it — would
demand a visibly different exponent. It does not.

### 2.3 The rival that survives, and why ImageNet-489 discriminates against it

The honest confound is **images per class**, which drops 5000 → 500 in the same step where C
rises 10 → 100. It is not fully separable in this corpus, and I am not claiming it is.

But it is already *partly* dead: CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet have **the same 500 images per
class** and Q differs between them by a factor of 1.7 (0.329 vs 0.194). Images-per-class alone
cannot produce that. Class count at fixed images-per-class can, and does.

And ImageNet-489 is a genuinely discriminating case, which is worth stating **before** the data
exists: it has 627,329 train images over 489 classes = **1283 images per class**, i.e. *more*
per class than CIFAR-100 or Tiny-ImageNet, while having 2.4–49× their class count.

* An **images-per-class law** predicts Q(489) recovers *upward*, back toward CIFAR-10's regime:
  **Q(489) > 0.33**, since 1283 > 500.
* The **class-count law** predicts Q(489) falls *further* than Tiny-ImageNet's 0.194.

These predictions do not overlap. `in489g1` separates them.

## 3. THE LAW, COMMITTED

> **THE CLASS-COUNT LAW.** In this campaign's matched HF configuration (ResNet-18 family,
> base SGDm, meta Lion, meta-stepsize 1e-3, α₀ 1e-6, γ 1, BETA_CLIP −15:−2.3026, AUGMENT=1,
> HIER unset, 100 epochs), the **scalar-retention ratio**
>
> ```
> Q(C) = M_scalar(C) / M_layerwise(C)        on plateau5 test accuracy
> ```
>
> is a decreasing power law in the number of classes C, and in C essentially alone:
>
> ```
> Q(C)  =  Q₁₀ · (C / 10)^(−b)        Q₁₀ = 0.9668,   b = 0.511
> ```
>
> Consequently the granularity gap
> `GRAN(C) = M_layerwise(C) − M_scalar(C) = M_layerwise(C) · (1 − Q(C))`
> is in the **tens of percentage points for every C ≥ 100**, and the underlying failure is a
> failure of the scalar arm to **fit the training set**, not to generalise from it.

Fit provenance: `Q₁₀` is the CIFAR-10 cell itself (0.9668). `b` is the least-squares slope of
`ln(Q/Q₁₀)` on `ln(C/10)` through the origin over the two remaining cells:
`b = Σxy/Σx² = 0.5111`. Per-transition endpoints are b = 0.4686 and b = 0.5363. Three cells
support two degrees of freedom, which is thin, and §7 says so plainly.

**Floor check, done in advance so it cannot be used as an excuse later.** Q is bounded below by
chance/M_layerwise. At C = 489 chance is 1/489 = 0.2045%, so if the layerwise arm lands anywhere
above ~20 pp the floor on Q is ≤ 0.010 — far below the value predicted next. The prediction is
therefore not floor-limited and cannot be trivially satisfied by both arms bottoming out.

## 4. THE NUMERIC PREDICTION FOR IMAGENET-489, MADE BEFORE ANY RESULT EXISTS

C = 489. `(489/10)^(−0.511) = 0.13697`, so:

### PRIMARY PREDICTION (scale-free; needs no forecast of absolute accuracy)

> **Q(489) = M_scalar / M_layerwise = 0.132**
> **registered 80% interval: [0.05, 0.30]**

The three fitted exponents alone span Q(489) ∈ [0.120, 0.156]. The interval is widened to
[0.05, 0.30] to carry what the exponent spread does not: three cells, three datasets, a
resolution jump from 64px to 224px, a change of augmentation pipeline, and 84 epochs instead of
100. The widening is stated now, not after.

Q is primary precisely because it needs no forecast of ImageNet-489's absolute accuracy. It
cannot be voided by the layerwise arm landing somewhere unexpected, so it cannot be quietly
rescued either.

### SECONDARY PREDICTION (pp, the scale cI1 scores on)

`GRAN(489) = M_layerwise(489) · (1 − 0.132) = 0.868 · M_layerwise(489)`, so:

> **GRAN(489) ≥ +15.0 pp — committed unconditionally.**
> Point estimate **+30 pp**, registered 80% interval **[+15, +48] pp**.

The pp scale carries an extra burden the law does not discharge: the absolute value of
`M_layerwise(489)`, which no part of this law predicts. The dependence is written out here so
it cannot be introduced later as a rescue: at Q = 0.132 the +15 pp floor requires
`M_layerwise(489) ≥ 17.3 pp`; at the pessimistic end of the Q interval (Q = 0.30) it requires
`M_layerwise(489) ≥ 21.4 pp`. **A layerwise arm below 21.4 pp does not falsify the law and does
not confirm it — it makes the pp prediction uninformative, and Q alone is scored.** Q is stated
first for exactly this reason.

### TERTIARY PREDICTIONS (free, and each independently checkable)

1. **Strict ordering.** `M_scalar < M_blocks < M_layerwise ≤ M_nodewise` at C = 489.
2. **Blockwise retention.** `M_blocks / M_layerwise ≈ 0.63`, interval [0.40, 0.90]. Fitted from
   two points only (CIFAR-10: 91.448/90.893 = 1.0061; CIFAR-100: 53.020/69.302 = 0.7651;
   log-log slope −0.1189), so this is the weakest number in the document and is labelled as such.
   Consequence: **BLOCK = M_blocks − M_scalar ≈ 0.50 · M_layerwise, i.e. also tens of pp and
   emphatically positive.** This directly contradicts the parent paper's ImageNet report of no
   improvement over scalar, on the parent's own blockwise-vs-scalar contrast.
3. **The mechanism is train-side.** `train5(scalar) / train5(layerwise) < 0.5` at C = 489, and
   the scalar arm's train and test accuracies agree to within a few pp of each other — it
   underfits rather than overfits, exactly as at C = 100 and C = 200.
4. **Extrapolation to full ImageNet-1k, recorded but not tested here:** Q(1000) = 0.092,
   interval [0.03, 0.25]. Noted so the law's commitment at the parent's actual scale is on
   record; `in489g1` does not test it.

## 5. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY THE LAW

Stated as bands, on the primary statistic Q, and fixed here.

| band | condition | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **CONFIRMED** | Q(489) ∈ [0.05, 0.30] | the quantitative power law holds at 489 classes |
| **DIRECTION-ONLY, LOW** | Q(489) < 0.05 | law's direction right, power law *under*-predicts the collapse; the functional form is wrong and must be refitted or replaced |
| **DIRECTION-ONLY, HIGH** | Q(489) ∈ (0.30, 0.60] | a large granularity gap is real but materially smaller than the law says; the functional form is wrong |
| **FALSIFIED** | **Q(489) > 0.60** | the class-count law is wrong. A scalar step size retains most of layerwise's accuracy at 489 classes, so class count does not control scalar failure |
| **DECISIVELY FALSIFIED** | **Q(489) ≥ 0.95** | this is the parent paper's own ImageNet claim ("no improvement over scalar"). The parent is right and this law is dead |

**Independent falsifiers, any one of which counts against the law on its own:**

* **The ordering breaks.** `M_scalar ≥ M_blocks`, or `M_layerwise < M_blocks` by more than
  0.5 pp, at C = 489. (Prediction §4-T1.)
* **The mechanism is not underfitting.** If the scalar arm at C = 489 fits its training set —
  `train5(scalar) > 0.7 · train5(layerwise)` — while still losing on test, then the phenomenon
  is a generalisation gap and §2.1's account of it is wrong, *even if Q lands in band*. This
  would make a correct Q prediction a coincidence, and it must be reported as such.
* **The rival wins.** Q(489) > 0.33 is simultaneously outside this law's interval and inside the
  images-per-class law's prediction (§2.3). That result is not merely a miss; it is positive
  evidence for the rival, and must be reported that way rather than as "unresolved".

**What does NOT count as falsification, registered now to close the escape hatches:**

* A low absolute `M_layerwise(489)`. That voids the pp prediction only (§4-secondary), never Q.
* `M_nodewise < M_layerwise`. The nodewise direction is not part of this law.
* A wide seed spread. That yields UNRESOLVED on cI1's own terms; it is not evidence for the law.
* Any post-hoc change of statistic, window, or scale. plateau5 and Q are fixed here.

**The law is not a prediction of everything.** It says nothing about: which partition is
optimal at any C, whether hierarchical/shrinkage pooling helps (nine mechanisms tested, nine
dead), the interior r-peak that survived G4, the meta-optimiser's identity, or any dataset
outside the ResNet-18 / SGDm+Lion / ms 1e-3 / α₀ 1e-6 cell. It makes one claim about one ratio
as one variable moves.

## 6. THE MECHANISTIC SKETCH THAT MOTIVATES TASK 2 — FLAGGED AS NOT YET TESTED

A scalar step size must serve the backbone and the classifier head with one number, and the
head's size is the one part of the network that scales with C. For a 512-wide ResNet-18 fc
layer the head holds `513·C` parameters against a ~11.18M backbone:

| C | head params (513·C) | share of total |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 5,130 | 0.046% |
| 100 | 51,300 | 0.457% |
| 200 | 102,600 | 0.91% |
| 489 | 250,857 | 2.19% |
| 1000 | 513,000 | 4.39% |

This is **arithmetic, not a verified fact about the live tree**, and it is recorded as such.
Task 2 must confirm the tensor ordering on the actual model via `named_parameters()` before
anything is built on it.

One free piece of corroboration already sits in the corpus and is worth registering here
because it was derived before Task 2 ran: `resnet18_blocks` maps to `[3,12,15,15,15,2]`, whose
**final group of size 2 is exactly the head** (fc.weight, fc.bias) — so blockwise already
isolates the head from the backbone. At CIFAR-100 it captures
`(53.020 − 22.776) / (69.302 − 22.776) = 30.244 / 46.526 = 65.0%` of the layerwise-minus-scalar
gap with m = 6 groups instead of 62. That is consistent with the head/backbone account and is
*not* proof of it, since blockwise splits the backbone five ways as well. The m = 2 experiment
in Task 2 is what separates those.

**This sketch is not part of the registered law.** The law in §3 stands or falls on Q(C) alone,
and would survive intact if the head account turned out to be the wrong mechanism.

## 7. WEAKNESSES, WRITTEN DOWN BEFORE THE DATA RATHER THAN AFTER

1. **Three cells, two free parameters.** The power law is not over-determined. It is a
   commitment, not a demonstration.
2. **The three cells are three different datasets.** C is confounded with everything else that
   differs between CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet. §2.2 and §2.3 narrow this as far as
   the corpus permits and no further. The clean experiment — one dataset, class count subsetted
   — has not been run, and this law would be much stronger if it had.
3. **Images per class is not fully separated** (§2.3). It is partly excluded by the
   CIFAR-100/Tiny-ImageNet pair at fixed 500/class, and ImageNet-489 at 1283/class discriminates
   further, but the 10 → 100 transition remains confounded.
4. **ImageNet-489 changes more than C.** 224px vs 32/64px, a different augmentation pipeline,
   batch 256 vs 100, 84 epochs vs 100, and a train set 49% the size of ImageNet-1k's. A miss
   could be any of these rather than the law. This is why §5's DIRECTION-ONLY bands exist and
   are reported honestly instead of being collapsed into pass/fail.
5. **Tiny-ImageNet's scalar arm at 9.9% is close to a degenerate regime.** It is 20× chance and
   is a genuine stall rather than a collapse (`collapsed` is not flagged and the run completed
   100 epochs), but a stalled arm carries less information than a training one, and the b = 0.536
   endpoint leans on it.
6. **n is small in the CIFAR-100 cell** — 5 scalar and 8 layerwise runs over 2–3 batches. The
   effect is 46.5 pp against a within-cell sd under 0.7 pp, so the gap is not in doubt; the
   *exact* value of Q(100) = 0.329 carries more uncertainty than three decimal places suggest.

## 9. DISAGREEMENTS WITH CIRCULATING PROSE, RECORDED RATHER THAN SMOOTHED

The briefing that prompted this work states the CIFAR-10 cell as
`scalar 86.441 (n=28), layerwise 90.641 (n=34), GRAN +4.201`. **I could not reproduce those
numbers under any predicate I tried**, and I am not using them. Under the matched predicate of
§1.1 the cell is `scalar 87.879 (n=12), layerwise 90.893 (n=17), GRAN +3.014`. Two checks
support the stricter figure over the briefing's:

* It reproduces, to four decimal places, the archived CIFAR-10 reference that
  `analysis/cI1_in489g1_score.py` derived independently and committed at `c7e032d` — 87.879 /
  91.448 / 90.893 / 91.739 and GRAN_CIFAR = +3.0134 — a scorer written by a different pass.
* The `tn1` in-batch CIFAR-10 layerwise control, re-parsed from raw logs on a different
  account, lands at 91.006, i.e. within 0.11 pp of 90.893 and 0.37 pp from 90.641.

Relaxing the predicate to `CIFAR10 / SGDm / Lion / ms 1e-3 / α₀ 1e-6 / hier ''` while dropping
the network, epoch-count, augmentation and beta-clip constraints gives n = 38 / 39 and
GRAN = +16.483 — so an n≈28/34 cell exists somewhere between the two, but it pools ResNet10,
ResNet34 and ResNet50 rows and 5-, 20- and 300-epoch runs, and is not config-matched to
`in489g1`. **The briefing's +4.201 is not used anywhere in this document.**

The briefing's CIFAR-100 numbers (22.776 n=5, 69.302 n=8) and Tiny-ImageNet numbers
(9.859 / 50.835 / 53.136, GRAN +40.975) **do** reproduce exactly. The direction and magnitude
of the class-count effect is unaffected by the CIFAR-10 discrepancy — under either figure,
GRAN rises by more than an order of magnitude from C = 10 to C = 100 at fixed resolution.
