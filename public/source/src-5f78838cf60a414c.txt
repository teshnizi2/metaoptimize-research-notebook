# MAIN-IDEA LEDGER — everything that was tried on hierarchical / partially-pooled step sizes

**Scope.** This document accounts for the ORIGINAL idea only: learn a scalar and a
layerwise/blockwise set of step sizes jointly, and shrink the fine ones toward the coarse one
with a tunable strength. It is not about the replacement paper (the count-matched partition
audit). Every number below was re-derived at write time from `results/all_runs.csv`
(2,177 rows) and, where the question is about a trajectory rather than an endpoint, from the
raw Slurm `.out` epoch series on cluster_a (`/cluster/account_a/metaopt/runs/`). No number is
quoted from prose, including from `docs/MASTER-TABLE.md`.

**Metric discipline.** `plateau5` (mean of the last 5 test epochs) is PRIMARY throughout.
The CSV column `plateau` is mean-of-last-20 and is BANNED as primary; `best_test` is not a
plateau at all. **Almost every headline number the project published about this idea is on
`plateau` or on `best_test`.** Where that is so, both values are given and the metric the
original claim used is named. Unless stated otherwise, every cell is filtered to
`superseded != 1`, `window_ok == 1`, `complete == 1`, with the 21 `dup_group` pairs collapsed
to one row each (4 of those 21 groups are hierarchical: three `h2-blk6/h2-b-L0p1` block pairs
and one `h2-l-L0p1_s2 / h2-lam01_s2` layerwise pair).

**Replication unit.** BATCH, not seed (`F(62,85)=5.47, p=6.9e-13`; seed `F=1.21, p=0.213`).
Seed-level standard errors below therefore UNDERSTATE uncertainty. The campaign's own
cross-batch floor is **0.18–0.29 pp** (`docs/CLOSEOUT.md` §items 5, 2). Any contrast smaller
than that, and any contrast whose two arms are drawn from different batch families, is not
resolved no matter what its t-statistic says. The batch families behind each cell are named
where it matters.

## 0. What was covered, and what it cost

`hier` column over the whole CSV:

| `hier` | rows | GPU-h (`wallclock_min`/60) | what it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `''` (not hierarchical) | 1,540 | 1,148.7 | the rest of the campaign |
| `additive` | 437 | 354.3 | M1 — the retention-ratio operator `r` |
| `shrink` | 113 | 66.4 | M0 — pull each group's beta toward the group mean, every step |
| `zpool` | 83 | 71.0 | the confound-free operator, pooling in meta-gradient space |
| `zmpool` | 4 | 1.0 | a variant that exists only as 4 twenty-epoch gate runs |
| **main idea, total** | **637** | **492.8** | 29.3% of runs, 30.0% of the campaign's 1,641.5 GPU-h |

After collapsing the 4 hierarchical duplicate rows: **633 runs / 490.2 GPU-h.** These are the
hierarchical ARMS only; the plain (`hier=''`) control arms they are differenced against are
shared with the rest of the campaign and are not charged here.

The 637 runs occupy **218 distinct configuration cells**
(`hier` x `lam` x `eta_ratio` x network x dataset x granularity x `alpha0` x base x meta x
epochs). Median n per cell = 3; **52 cells are n=1** and 45 are n=2.

**One axis is entirely unexplored: all 637 hierarchical runs sit at `meta_stepsize = 1e-3`.**
That matters more than anything else in this document, because the central mechanism finding
(§M1-5) is that `r` *is* a meta-stepsize dial. The baselines were meta-stepsize-tuned; the
hierarchical arms never were.

**Operators, exactly as implemented** (`patches/HF_patched.py:380-410`, `patches/patch_zpool.py`):

* **M0 `shrink`** — after each meta-step, `beta <- beta - lam * (beta - mean(beta))`.
  Applied EVERY step. Contraction factor per step `(1-lam)`.
* **M1 `additive`** — `beta <- beta_prev + mean(d) + r * (d - mean(d))`, where
  `d = beta - beta_prev` is the realised increment. `r=1` is the identity; `r=0` gives every
  group the shared increment. Acts on the realised increment, i.e. AFTER the meta-optimizer.
* **`zpool`** — `z'_b = (1-r) * sum_j z_j + r * z_b` on the meta-GRADIENT, i.e. BEFORE the
  meta-optimizer. Both endpoints are exact identities: `r=0` is exactly the scalar arm,
  `r=1` is exactly the plain per-group arm.

CIFAR-10 at batch 100 is 500 optimiser steps/epoch, so a 100-epoch run is **50,000 steps**
and a 300-epoch run is 150,000.

## 1. M0 SHRINK — the proposal's own operator

### M0-1. Is there a tunable shrink coefficient with an interior optimum?

* **Varied:** `lam` over {1e-5, 3e-5, 1e-4, 1e-3, 0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1.0}.
* **Scale:** ResNet18 / CIFAR-10 / layerwise / `alpha0=1e-6` / SGDm base + Lion meta /
  `ms=1e-3` / `beta_clip=-15:-2.3026` / augment=1 / batch 100 / 100 epochs.
  n=2 on the three lowest rungs (`lp-*`), n=3 elsewhere, n=11 at `lam=0.1`.
  Batch families: `lp` (1e-5, 3e-5, 1e-4), `h2` (1e-3, 0.01, 0.03, 0.5, 1.0),
  `h2`+`hs`+`a0L`+`a0h` (0.1), `h2` (0.3).
* **Result (re-derived, plateau5 | plateau | best_test):**

| `lam` | n | plateau5 | plateau (banned) | best_test | plateau5 vs plain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| — (plain layerwise) | 17 | 90.893 ±0.191 | 90.784 | 91.200 ±0.179 | — |
| 1e-5 | 2 | 90.914 ±0.124 | 90.803 | 91.370 | +0.021 |
| 3e-5 | 2 | 90.958 ±0.034 | 90.790 | 91.295 | +0.065 |
| 1e-4 | 2 | 90.761 ±0.219 | 90.615 | 91.035 | −0.132 |
| 1e-3 | 3 | 92.426 ±0.168 | 92.324 | 92.543 | **+1.533** |
| 0.01 | 3 | **92.575 ±0.176** | 92.472 | 92.787 | **+1.682** |
| 0.03 | 3 | 92.229 ±0.148 | 92.246 | 92.583 | +1.336 |
| 0.1 | 11 | 92.321 ±0.105 | 92.293 | 92.594 | +1.428 |
| 0.3 | 3 | 92.274 ±0.226 | 92.224 | 92.507 | +1.381 |
| 0.5 | 3 | 92.255 ±0.133 | 92.196 | 92.500 | +1.362 |
| 1.0 | 3 | 92.257 ±0.191 | 92.199 | 92.533 | +1.364 |

  The ladder is a **step function, not a dial**. Below `lam=1e-3` the operator does nothing
  (the three lowest rungs sit inside the plain arm's own seed band). At and above `lam=1e-3`
  it is flat: one-way ANOVA over the seven rungs `lam` in [1e-3, 1.0] gives
  **F(6,22)=2.138, p=0.089** (N=29), span 0.346 pp against a pooled within-cell sd of 0.148 —
  and the span is itself below the 0.20–0.29 pp cross-batch floor. `lam=1.0` (full pooling)
  ranks **fifth of seven on plateau5** and sixth of seven on best_test; it is not the optimum
  and it is not distinguishable from any other rung above 1e-3.
* **Verdict: WITHDRAWN.**
  Not REFUTED, because the question was never actually put. The operator has one live decade,
  [1e-4, 1e-3], and **zero runs sit inside it**. Above it the operator saturates; below it
  the operator is inert. WITHDRAWN is the honest label for a claim whose supporting sweep
  measured the same thing seven times.
* **So what:** *hierarchical shrinkage with a tunable coefficient — the actual proposal — was
  never tested.* Everything the project reported under the name "M0 shrink" is a measurement
  of FULL pooling of the beta increments, reported seven times at seven labels.

### M0-2. Why it saturates — the half-life arithmetic, checked against the data

* **Varied:** nothing; this is the operator's own dynamics, cross-checked against M0-1.
* **Result:** the shrink half-life in steps is `ln 2 / (-ln(1-lam))`: 6,931 steps at
  `lam=1e-4`; **693 steps at `lam=1e-3`**; 69 at 0.01; 6.6 at 0.1; 1 at 0.5. A 100-epoch run
  is 50,000 steps, so every `lam >= 1e-3` runs for **at least 72 half-lives**. The data agree
  on the upper end and locate the knee one decade higher than pure contraction predicts:
  `lam=1e-4` (7.2 half-lives, 99.3% contraction if the meta-gradient did nothing) still reads
  as plain, because the meta-gradient re-separates the betas every step. The operator is
  therefore a competition between shrinkage and re-separation whose transition sits somewhere
  in [1e-4, 1e-3].
* **Verdict: CONFIRMED** (saturation above 1e-3) **and NEVER-TESTED** (the transition decade).
* **So what:** the sweep design was wrong by one decade at the bottom and three decades too
  wide at the top. Six of the ten rungs run were uninformative by construction.

### M0-3. How large is the shrink effect, and against what?

* **Varied:** the reference arm.
* **Record:** deltas "+1.17 to +1.46 pp", on `best_test`, against "plain layerwise
  91.33 ±0.17 (n=6)".
* **Re-derived:** the 13-axis-matched plain layerwise cell is **n=17**, `best_test`
  91.200 ±0.179, `plateau5` 90.893 ±0.191, `plateau` 90.784 (batch families
  `a0L/a0h/h2/mx/sc/zad`). The n=6 reference in the record cannot be uniquely reconstructed
  from the CSV under any single-family or clip/augment filter. Against the matched cell the
  deltas are **+1.30 to +1.59 pp on best_test** and **+1.34 to +1.68 pp on plateau5**.
  Welch against the n=17 arm, plateau5: `lam=0.01` +1.682, se 0.112, t=15.07;
  `lam=1.0` +1.364, se 0.120, t=11.41.
* **Verdict: CONFIRMED as an effect; WITHDRAWN as evidence for the idea.**
* **So what:** the effect is real and larger than published, but it is the effect of ONE
  setting (full pooling of increments), and §M1-5 shows that setting is a meta-stepsize
  reduction. Quoting the ladder's peak (+1.46, or +1.68 on the corrected reference) was and
  remains a cherry-pick over a flat ladder.

### M0-4. Does shrink survive a non-crippled initialisation?

* **Varied:** `alpha0` in {1e-6, 1e-4, 1e-3} at `lam=0.1`, under BOTH meta-optimizers
  (batches `a0L`, `a0h`, `a0A`, `h2A`).
* **Scale:** R18/CIFAR-10, layerwise, 100 ep, n=2–11 per cell.
* **Result (plateau5, shrink vs its own matched plain arm):**

| meta | `alpha0` | shrink `lam=0.1` | plain layerwise | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lion | 1e-6 | 92.321 (n=11) | 90.893 (n=17) | **+1.428** |
| Lion | 1e-4 | 92.273 (n=4) | 90.949 (n=9) | **+1.324** |
| Lion | 1e-3 | 92.226 (n=4) | 91.196 (n=14) | **+1.030** |
| Adam | 1e-6 | 91.936 (n=6) | 90.446 (n=9) | **+1.490** |
| Adam | 1e-4 | 91.750 (n=2) | 90.955 (n=2) | +0.795 |
| Adam | 1e-3 | 92.008 (n=2) | 91.287 (n=2) | +0.721 |

* **Verdict: CONFIRMED** — unlike M1's interior optimum (§M1-4), the M0 effect does NOT
  vanish at a proper init; it attenuates by ~30% and stays above the 0.5 pp materiality bar
  under Lion at every `alpha0`.
* **So what:** this is the one place the M0 arm looks better than the M1 arm, and it is worth
  saying plainly to avoid the impression that everything collapsed for the same reason. It
  does not rescue the idea, because §M1-5 shows the same +1.0 pp is bought more cheaply and

### M0-5. Does the shrink gain survive a longer budget?

* **Varied:** budget window E in {100, 205, 300} epochs, read off ONE 300-epoch trajectory per
  seed (so this is three windows of one run, not three independent budgets — the defect
  `CORRECTIONS 86.1` names).
* **Scale:** R18/CIFAR-10, layerwise, `lam=0.1`, 300 epochs, n=2 seeds per arm, two families:
  `e300-*` at `alpha0=1e-3` and `e3a-*` at `alpha0=1e-6`. Re-derived directly from the raw
  `.out` epoch series (`e300-lsh01-s{0,1}`, `e300-lplain-s{0,1}`, `e3a-lsh01-s{0,1}`,
  `e3a-lplain-s{0,1}`), plateau5 = mean of epochs E−5..E−1.
* **Result (shrink `lam=0.1` minus plain layerwise, plateau5):**

| family | `alpha0` | E=100 | E=205 | E=300 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `e300` | 1e-3 | **+0.682** | +0.271 | +0.158 |
| `e3a` | 1e-6 | **+1.605** | +0.535 | **+0.062** |

  For contrast, the granularity effect on the same trajectories does not decay
  (`e300-lplain` minus `e300-scal`: +3.505 / +3.486 / +3.413 at E=100/205/300).
* **Verdict: REFUTED.** The pooling gain is a speed effect. At `alpha0=1e-6` it is gone by
  300 epochs (+0.062 pp, well inside one seed band).
* **So what:** the parent paper's ImageNet null is a LONG-BUDGET null. An intervention whose
  benefit halves every ~100 epochs is exactly the wrong shape to fix it. This single result,
  n=2 though it is, is the most direct evidence against the idea's stated purpose.

### M0-7. Does shrink transfer to CIFAR-100?

* **Varied:** dataset, at `lam=0.01`, `alpha0` in {1e-6, 1e-3} (batch `m0c`).
* **Scale:** ResNet18_c100 / CIFAR-100 / layerwise / 100 ep / n=2–3.

### M1-2. Does the identity control hold?

* **Varied:** `r=1`, which is algebraically the plain arm.
* **Result:** plateau5 90.971 ±0.129 (n=3, `zad`) vs plain layerwise 90.893 ±0.191 (n=17):

### M1-3. Is `r=0` actually full pooling? (the granularity-invariance test)

* **Varied:** granularity at `r=0` across 180,000x in group count `m`, with `zpool r=0` as

  interior does, and what is left is §M1-5's meta-stepsize effect.

### M1-5. Is `r` a POOLING dial or a META-STEPSIZE dial? — the decisive test

* **The mechanism.** Under Lion every realised beta increment is exactly `+-ms`, so the shared
  component `mean_b(d_b) = ms*(2p-1)` where `p` is the cross-group sign agreement. Moving `r`
  from 1 to 0 therefore divides the effective meta-stepsize by `|2p-1|`. Measured
  `|2p-1|` per granularity (from `results/p7_c31.txt`, the `p7` steady window — this is a
  probe measurement, NOT re-derivable from the CSV, and is reported here as record):
  0.3947 (blk6, m=6) / 0.1277 (layerwise, 62) / 0.0152 (nodewise, 14,420) / 0.000331
  (weightwise, 11.2M) — strictly monotone over six decades of `m`, giving `ms_eff` from
  3.9e-4 down to 3.3e-7. `zpool`, which acts BEFORE Lion, has no such factor: its `ms_eff`
  is `ms` at every `r`.
* **The decisive contrast, fully axis-matched.** R18/CIFAR-10, `alpha0=1e-3`, SGDm+Lion,
  augment=1, clip, batch 100, gamma=1, 100 epochs — everything identical except the two knobs
  under test:

| arm | `ms` | n | plateau5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| plain scalar | 1e-3 | 14 | 87.839 ±0.240 |
| plain layerwise | 1e-3 | 14 | 91.196 ±0.193 |
| `additive` r=0 (full pooling) | 1e-3 | 7 | 92.217 ±0.075 |
| `additive` r=0.1 (best interior) | 1e-3 | 7 | 92.403 ±0.139 |
| **plain scalar, meta-stepsize tuned** | **1e-4** | 5 | **92.262 ±0.222** |
| **plain layerwise, meta-stepsize tuned** | **1e-4** | 11 | **92.887 ±0.173** |

  A plain SCALAR step size with the meta-stepsize turned down one decade **matches the best
  hierarchical arm**: `additive` r=0.1 minus tuned scalar = **+0.140 pp, se 0.112, t=1.25** —
  not significant, and below the cross-batch floor. And a plain LAYERWISE arm with the same
  one-decade meta-stepsize tune **beats** it: `additive` r=0.1 minus tuned layerwise =

  Corroborating: plain scalar run at exactly the `ms_eff` the mechanism predicts for
  additive-r=0-layerwise (1.277e-4) reads 91.849 ±0.131 (n=5); the whole scalar ms-response

  3e-5, 90.838 at 1e-5, 90.451 at 1e-8).
* **Verdict: REFUTED.**
* **So what: THE HIERARCHICAL IDEA'S MECHANISM IS DEAD.** `r` is a meta-learning-rate dial
  wearing a pooling costume. At a proper initialisation, everything the operator buys is
  bought — and beaten — by turning one existing scalar knob. There is no diagnosis of "why
  the expressive parameterisation loses" left to write, because the fine parameterisation was
  never the thing being adjusted.

### M1-12. Is there a scale law for the optimum, `r* ~ 1/N`?

* **Varied:** `r*` across R10 / R18 / R34 at `alpha0=1e-6`; `r34f` added the sub-0.02 rungs
  specifically to bracket R34 downward.
* **Result (plateau5):** r* = 0.10 (R10, 4.90M) / 0.06 (R18, 11.17M) / 0.025 (R34, 21.28M),
  giving `r* x N` = 0.490M / 0.670M / 0.532M, log-log slope −0.930, R^2 0.952. But every one
  of the three argmaxes is decided by a CROSS-FAMILY margin far below the floor: R34
  r=0.025 (`r34f`) beats r=0.02 (`r34r`) by 0.055 pp and r=0.03 by 0.118 pp; R18 r=0.06 beats
  r=0.07 by 0.063 pp across families {`mx`,`sc`} vs {`ad`,`mx`,`zad`}; R10 r=0.1 beats r=0.08
  by 0.124 pp across `r10` vs `r10b`.
* **Verdict: OPEN.**
* **So what:** `r*` falls monotonically with N and any slope in roughly [−1.1, −0.8] fits, but
  the grid spacing, not the seed count, is the binding limit, and the scope is `alpha0=1e-6`
  only (at 1e-3 R10's interior optimum disappears entirely). Left open deliberately: a scale
  law for a dial that turned out to be a meta-learning-rate knob is not worth compute.

### M1-13. Was the M1 arm's own meta-stepsize ever tuned?

  versus best-over-`ms`, and half of that grid does not exist. The comparison in §M1-5 —
  which the hierarchical arm loses — is against a baseline tuned on an axis the hierarchical
  arm was never given.

## 3. zpool — the confound-free operator

### Z-1. Do the endpoints reproduce their identities?

* **Result (plateau5 | plateau):** layerwise `r=1` 91.037 ±0.176 (n=5) | 90.933, vs plain
  layerwise 90.893 | 90.784 — +0.144 | +0.149. Weightwise `r=1` 79.235 ±0.345 (n=5) | 77.921,

  87.943 | 87.763.
* **Verdict: CONFIRMED.** This is the only operator in the programme whose both endpoints are
  exact, and they verify.
* **So what:** `zpool` is the instrument the other two should have been. Everything measured
  with it is trustworthy in a way the M0/M1 numbers are not.

### Z-2. Does true full pooling help?

* **Result:** `zpool r=0` 87.880 ±0.157 (n=5, families `zsw`+`zsx`) vs plain layerwise
  90.893 ±0.191 (n=17) = **−3.013 pp**, Welch se 0.084, t=−35.90. On the banned `plateau`

* **Verdict: REFUTED.**
* **So what:** the claim that "even full pooling helps, +1.39 pp" — carried in the handoff
  document for roughly twenty cycles — read the `additive r=0` cell, which §M1-3 shows is not
  a pooling arm. Under the operator that actually pools, full pooling costs 3 pp.

### Z-3. Does true pooling have an interior optimum at a good partition?

* **Varied:** `r` in {0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99, 1}, layerwise.
* **Scale:** R18/CIFAR-10, a0=1e-6, 100 ep, n=1–6 (families `zsw`, `zsx`, `zrn`).
* **Result (plateau5 | plateau):** 87.880 | 87.740 (r=0) / 89.728 | 89.471 / 89.677 | 89.476 /
  89.361 | 89.170 / 89.235 | 89.100 / 90.335 | 90.196 / 90.454 | 90.435 /

* **Verdict: REFUTED.**
* **So what:** with the meta-stepsize confound removed, there is no interior optimum at

### Z-4. Does true pooling rescue an over-fine partition?

* **Varied:** the same `r` ladder at weightwise.
* **Result (plateau5 | plateau):** 87.943 | 87.763 (r=0) / 87.986 / 88.094 / 88.004 / 88.256 /
  88.614 / 88.905 / **89.272 ±0.229 | 89.052 (r=0.99, n=2)** / 79.235 | 77.921 (r=1).
  The rescue is **+10.037 pp on plateau5** (Welch se 0.224, t=44.85) — the record's +11.13 pp
  is the same contrast on the banned `plateau`, inflated by 1.09 pp because the plain
  weightwise arm is still climbing at epoch 80.
* **Verdict: CONFIRMED as a rescue, REFUTED as a fix.** The best true-pooling number anywhere
  (89.272) is still **2.18 pp below plain `resnet18_blocks`** (91.448 ±0.168, n=12) and
  1.62 pp below plain layerwise. It clears plain scalar by only +1.393 pp.
* **So what:** the precise, defensible sentence about pooling is: *pooling rescues an
  over-fine partition and never improves a good one.* You cannot get to a good partition by
  pooling a bad one; you get there by choosing a good partition.
* **Caveat the record already makes:** the INTERIOR of the zpool sweep is itself confounded —
  the shared term carries a factor of `m`, so interior cells train at 3–35x smaller effective
  step size. Only the two endpoints are clean.

### Z-5. What zpool never covered

* **Granularity:** only layerwise and weightwise. **No `zpool` run exists at `nodewise` or
  `resnet18_blocks`** — i.e. the operator was never applied at the two partitions that
  actually perform well.
* **Initialisation:** 79 of 83 runs at `alpha0=1e-6`. The only `alpha0=1e-3` zpool runs are
  4 twenty-epoch gates (`z3-*`, `window_ok=0`).
* **Architecture / dataset:** ResNet18 / CIFAR-10 only. Zero transfer runs.
* **Budget:** 100 epochs only. Zero long-budget runs.
* **Verdict: NEVER-TESTED** on all four axes.
* **So what:** the one trustworthy operator got 83 runs and 71 GPU-h, against 437 runs and
  354 GPU-h for the operator that turned out to be measuring something else. The programme
  spent 5x more compute on the confounded instrument than on the clean one, and built the
  clean one last.

## 6. The gap list — what was never tried

Ordered by how much it would have changed the conclusion. This is the part that is most
worth arguing with.

1. **The proposal's own regime: `lam` in (1e-4, 1e-3).** Zero runs. This is the ONLY decade in
   which M0's shrink coefficient does anything other than nothing or everything. Cost to
   close: ~6 runs (3 seeds x 2 rungs at 3e-4 and 6e-4), ~4 GPU-h. **Nothing in this programme
   is cheaper or more directly aimed at the original question.**
2. **The hierarchical arms' own meta-stepsize.** All 637 runs at `ms=1e-3`. Since `r` is a
   meta-stepsize dial, the only fair comparison is best-over-(`r`,`ms`) vs best-over-`ms`, and
   the first grid does not exist. Cost: ~30 runs.
3. **`zpool` at a good partition.** No `zpool` run at `nodewise` or `resnet18_blocks`. Every
   trustworthy pooling number in the corpus is at layerwise or weightwise. Cost: ~30 runs.
4. **`zpool` at `alpha0=1e-3`.** Four 20-epoch gates only. The entire clean-operator story is
   measured inside the crippled-init regime that §M1-4 showed manufactures the effect.
5. **`zmpool`, entirely.** 4 gate runs, 1 GPU-h.
6. **The Lion-base inversion (§M1-10).** n=2, one family, and it says the optimum moves to the
   boundary when the base optimizer changes. If true it is a mechanism result; it was never
   followed up.
7. **M1's budget behaviour.** One trajectory pair, n=2, no matched plain arm at E=205/300.
   M0's decay is measured; M1's is not.
8. **ResNet34 at `alpha0=1e-3`.** One cell (r=0.06, n=3), no curve. The `alpha0` control that
   killed the R18 optimum was never run on the architecture where the optimum is sharpest.
9. **Any dataset other than CIFAR-10/100.** No ImageNet, no downscaled ImageNet, nothing.
   The idea was proposed to explain an ImageNet result and was never tested at a scale where
   that result appears.
10. **A shrink operator applied on a SCHEDULE rather than every step.** The saturation in
    §M0-2 is a consequence of applying the contraction 50,000 times. An operator applied
    once per epoch, or with a strength annealed over training, is a different operator and
    was never built.

---

## 7. Bottom line

637 runs and 492.8 GPU-hours went into the main idea, across four operators.

* The **empirical curve is real** (M1-1): an interior optimum at r ~ 0.05–0.07 on
  ResNet18/CIFAR-10, +0.997 pp over its better endpoint, and it reproduces on ResNet10 and
  ResNet34.
* The **mechanism is not** (M1-5). Under Lion the additive operator divides the effective
  meta-stepsize by `|2p-1|`; a plain SCALAR step size with the meta-stepsize turned down one
  decade matches the best hierarchical arm to +0.140 pp (t=1.25), and a plain LAYERWISE arm
  with the same tune beats it by 0.484 pp (t=−6.53), on a fully axis-matched cell.
* Under the **operator that actually pools** (zpool), full pooling costs 3.0 pp, there is no
  interior optimum at a good partition, and the best true-pooling number anywhere is 2.2 pp
  below plain blk6.
* The **proposal's own knob was never turned.** M0's shrink coefficient has one live decade,
  [1e-4, 1e-3], and no run sits in it. Everything published as "M0 shrink with tunable lambda"
  is full pooling measured seven times.
* And at its own best point the whole stack **loses to AdamW + cosine** by 0.86–2.37 pp on
  ResNet18/34/50.

The honest summary is not "the idea failed". It is: **the idea was never tested in the form it
was proposed, and the two operators that WERE tested for 550 runs turned out to be measuring
the meta-learning rate.** The one clean instrument was built last, given 83 runs, and never
applied to a partition that works.
