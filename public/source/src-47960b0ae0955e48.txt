# MAIN-IDEA-GAPS — what was never tried on the hierarchical/shrinkage idea, and why

**Scope.** This document is the accounting for **Arsalan's November-2025 proposal only** — learn a
scalar and a layerwise step size jointly and shrink the layerwise ones toward the scalar with a
tunable strength, in order to diagnose why the strictly more expressive parameterisation loses on
ImageNet. It is **not** about the paper that exists instead (the count-matched partition audit).
It answers one question: *what did we not run, and was that a decision or an oversight.*

**Metric rule, applied throughout.** `plateau5` (mean of the last 5 test epochs) is primary. The
CSV `plateau` column is mean-of-last-20 and is not used. `best_test` is used only where it is
named explicitly, and where a recorded headline was a `best_test` number that is said so.

**Replication rule, applied throughout.** BATCH is the unit of replication in this corpus; SEED is
null. Every contrast below is **within one batch** unless it says otherwise, and where a contrast
is cross-batch that is flagged as a defect, not a result. `batch` is the run-name prefix up to the
first `-` or `_`. The 4 duplicate groups that touch hierarchical rows (8 rows) are collapsed before
any `n` is quoted.

**Every number below was re-derived at this HEAD from `results/all_runs.csv` (2,177 rows, 2,174
after dropping the 3 `superseded=1` rows) and, for the code and dataset claims, from the live tree
on cluster_a. Nothing is quoted from prose, including from the briefing that commissioned this
document — where my re-derivation disagrees with the record, §3.4 and §2.6 say so.** No Slurm job
was submitted; both queues were confirmed empty (`squeue -u account_a` returns a header only).

Reproduction preamble for every table in this file:


## 1. G1 — the proposal as literally written was never run

### 1.1 What the code does

`patches/HF_patched.py`, `_apply_hier`, reached from `step()` on **every minibatch**:

```python
if self._hier == 'shrink':
    self.beta[0] = b - self._hier_lam * (b - b.mean())
```

So the deviation of each group's log step size from the group mean is multiplied by (1−λ) once per
optimizer step. Over T steps the surviving deviation is (1−λ)^T. CIFAR-10 has 50,000 training
images and `batch_size = 100` in every run in the corpus, so a 100-epoch run is **50,000 meta
steps**.

### 1.2 The ladder, re-derived, within one batch

The only λ ladder that exists is ResNet-18 / CIFAR-10 / SGDm+Lion / layerwise / α₀=1e-6 / 100 ep.
Rungs λ ≥ 1e-3 are batch `h2`, which also carries its own plain-layerwise control (`h2-base_*`,
n=5, plateau5 **90.802**). Duplicate group `h2-layer-shrink0.1-s2` collapsed.

| λ | n | plateau5 | Δ vs `h2` plain | half-life (steps) | half-lives in 50,000 steps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.01 | 3 | 92.575 | +1.773 | 69.0 | 725 |
| 0.001 | 3 | 92.426 | +1.624 | 692.8 | 72.2 |
| 0.1 | 3 | 92.336 | +1.534 | 6.6 | 7,600 |
| 0.3 | 3 | 92.274 | +1.472 | 1.9 | 25,729 |
| **1.0** | 3 | 92.257 | +1.455 | 0 | ∞ |
| 0.5 | 3 | 92.255 | +1.453 | 1.0 | 50,000 |
| 0.03 | 3 | 92.229 | +1.427 | 22.8 | 2,197 |

Three points, all re-derived:

1. **The ladder is flat.** Over three decades of λ the spread is **0.346 pp** (92.229 → 92.575),
   against a within-cell seed SD of ≈0.20. There is no dose–response.
2. **Every rung is saturated.** The least aggressive rung on this ladder, λ=1e-3, still runs
   **72 half-lives**. By the end of *epoch 2* it is at full pooling. The sweep measured full
   pooling seven times.
3. **λ=1.0 ranks 5th of 7 on plateau5** — not 6th. The "sixth of seven" in the record is a
   `best_test` ranking. Either way the ordering is noise; the point is that it is noise, so
   "the optimum is at full pooling" was correctly struck and nothing replaces it.

### 1.3 The three genuinely partial rungs exist — in a different batch, with no control

λ ∈ {1e-5, 3e-5, 1e-4} exist **only** in batch `lp`, which contains **6 runs and nothing else** —
no plain arm, no scalar arm, no λ=1 arm.

| λ | batch | n | plateau5 | half-lives in 50,000 | deviation surviving at end |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1e-5 | `lp` | 2 | 90.914 | 0.72 | 61 % |
| 3e-5 | `lp` | 2 | 90.958 | 2.16 | 22 % |
| 1e-4 | `lp` | 2 | 90.761 | 7.21 | 0.7 % |

So the *only* three measurements of partial pooling on layerwise sit in an isolated 6-run batch,
and the 1.4-pp step between λ=1e-4 (`lp`, 90.761) and λ=1e-3 (`h2`, 92.426) is **perfectly
confounded with the `lp` → `h2` batch change**, which in this corpus is the established unit of
replication. That comparison cannot be made. It was made anyway.

Note also that even λ=1e-4 is effectively saturated (0.7 % deviation left). Genuinely partial
pooling at the end of a 100-epoch run needs λ ≲ 3e-5, i.e. **two rungs of one ladder in one batch
with no control**. That is the entire evidence base for the proposal's actual claim.

### 1.4 What the correct experiment would have been

The briefing asks precisely this. Four candidate repairs, and only two of them work:

* **(a) Prox / penalty form — the right one.** Add a quadratic penalty λ‖β − β̄‖² to the *meta*
  objective, so the meta-gradient carries a −2λ(β − β̄) term and the shrink is balanced against
  the meta-gradient's drive instead of applied unconditionally. The stationary deviation is then
  set by the ratio of the meta-gradient's between-group spread to λ — a genuine strength dial with
  **no time constant and no saturation**, and λ=0 is exactly plain by construction. This is what
  "partial pooling with a tunable coefficient" means in the statistics literature the proposal
  borrows from, and it is a ~15-line change inside `_apply_hier` (add to `HtT_gradft` before
  `meta_update`, not to `beta` after it).
  **Cost:** patch + a λ=0 byte-equivalence test (0 GPU) · 8 rungs × 3 seeds × 2 α₀ = 48 runs,
  plus in-batch plain and scalar controls at both α₀ (12 runs) = **60 runs ≈ 42 GPU-h, one batch.**

* **(b) Two-level parameterisation — the most faithful one.** Carry β_scalar and δ_g explicitly,
  β_g = β_scalar + δ_g, and give δ its own meta-step-size η_δ = ρ·η. ρ is the pooling strength;
  ρ=0 is *exactly* scalar and ρ=1 is per-group with the shared level still learned. No projection,
  no time constant. This is arguably what the proposal literally describes ("learn a scalar and
  layerwise step sizes **jointly**") and **no patch for it was ever written**.
  **Cost:** ~40 lines + an equivalence suite at both endpoints · 9 rungs × 3 seeds × 2 α₀ +
  controls = **66 runs ≈ 46 GPU-h.** This is G2.

* **(c) Periodic *full* shrink (λ=1 every k steps) — also valid.** Deviation is zeroed every k
  steps and rebuilt over the next k. This is a stationary process; k is a genuine, non-saturating
  strength dial (large k → nearly plain, k=1 → fully pooled). Cheapest of the three: one counter
  and an env var.
  **Cost:** k ∈ {1, 10, 100, 1000, 10000, ∞} × 3 seeds × 2 α₀ = **36 runs ≈ 25 GPU-h.**

* **(d) Periodic *partial* shrink (λ<1 every k steps) — does NOT work, and it is the obvious
  wrong answer.** It only reparameterises the per-step coefficient (λ_eff ≈ λ/k); the deviation
  still decays geometrically and still saturates, just later. Same for a per-epoch shrink with
  λ<1 (that is (d) with k=500). I flag this explicitly because it is the fix that will be
  proposed first, and it would waste a batch.

**Judgement.** This is the gap that matters. Until (a) or (b) is run, "hierarchical shrinkage
toward the scalar does not help" is **not a measured statement** — what was measured is "full
pooling of β, applied as a projection, gives +1.5 pp over plain layerwise at α₀=1e-6 on
ResNet-18, and is insensitive to how fast you get there."

`chunk*` family, and it is empty.

single-batch. Every other row is either single-batch or, where two batches supply it, holds
single-batch as well: within `zad` alone the R18-layerwise curve is 92.326 (r=0) → **93.201** (r=0.07)

the R18-nodewise, R10 and CIFAR-100 curves are each one batch throughout.

The ResNet-34 curve is the cleanest in the corpus: 14 rungs, unimodal, n=3 each, and the two
batches supplying it (`r34f`, `r34r`) **interleave** across the rungs, so the shape is not
batch-confounded. Its r=1 endpoint (90.421) also lands on the independent plain-layerwise measurement
in batch `sc` (90.178), which is the identity check the operator must pass.

**The honest statement is therefore narrower and more specific than "transfers nowhere":** the
interior optimum replicates on **4 of 7** tested (network × granularity) cells at α₀=1e-6, fails on
all of them at α₀=1e-3, fails on `weightwise` and `resnet18_blocks` at either α₀, and **inverts**
under a Lion base (where the optimum is at **full** pooling, r=0, giving the highest hierarchical
cell mean on ResNet-18 anywhere in the corpus, 93.925 at n=2 — the highest single hierarchical run
in the corpus is 94.092, `r34r-r002-s2` on ResNet-34). What does not transfer is (i) the *value* of r —
0.025 on R34, 0.06 on R18, 0.1 on R10 — and (ii) the whole phenomenon out of the α₀=1e-6 regime.
The record's α₀ argument still lands; its phrasing does not. Someone should fix the phrasing before
Saber checks it, because he will.

## 3. G3, G5, G10 — the motivating phenomenon

### 3.1 Was the anomaly ever reproduced? Yes — on CIFAR-10, in three batches

The idea exists to explain why layerwise loses to scalar on ImageNet. **ImageNet was never run**
(§3.6). But the anomaly itself — layerwise materially below scalar — **does reproduce at
CIFAR resolution**, and this appears not to have been read off the corpus in one place before.

Batch-matched Δ = plateau5(layerwise) − plateau5(scalar), 100 epochs, over all 45 cells in the
corpus that contain both arms in the same batch (43 such cells). The negative tail:

| batch | network | base+meta | α₀ | layerwise | scalar | Δ | n/arm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `a0` | ResNet-18 | AdamW + Adam | 1e-3 | 90.818 | 92.707 | **−1.889** | 3 |
| `tc1` | ResNet-18 | AdamW + Adam | 1e-3 | 91.190 | 92.751 | **−1.561** | 2 |
| `sc50` | **ResNet-50** | SGDm + Lion | 1e-6 | 88.154 | 89.510 | **−1.356** | 2 |
| `pp` | ResNet-18 | AdamW + Adam | 1e-6 | 73.400 | 73.849 | −0.449 | 3 |
| `PP` | ResNet-18 | AdamW + Adam | 1e-6 | 73.498 | 73.811 | −0.313 | 3 |
| `bo` | ResNet-18 | Lion + Lion | 1e-6 | 91.281 | 91.452 | −0.171 | 3 |
| `g1` | ResNet-18 | AdamW + Lion | 1e-6 | 91.511 | 91.616 | −0.105 | 3 |

The other 36 cells are positive, up to +55.6 pp on ResNet-10/CIFAR-100. So the anomaly is **real,
reproducible, and conditional** — it needs either the parent paper's own AdamW+Adam pair at
α₀=1e-3, or ResNet-50. It is *not* a property of scale alone: in the same `sc50` batch at α₀=1e-3
the sign flips (layerwise 90.375 vs scalar 89.245, **+1.130**). And `I1`, nominally the same
AdamW+Adam/α₀=1e-3 cell as `a0`, gives **+0.136** — so even within one configuration the anomaly is
batch-conditional and that discrepancy was never chased either.

**This is the most useful thing in this document for the project's own sake.** The premise is
testable at CIFAR cost. Nobody had to wait for ImageNet.

### 3.2 And the fix was almost never run where the anomaly lives

| anomaly cell | hierarchical runs in it | what exists |
|---|---|---|
| `a0` / `tc1`, R18, AdamW+Adam, α₀=1e-3 | **5** (batch `dc`) | `additive` r=0.06 **only**, imported from the SGDm+Lion optimum. No ladder, no `shrink`, and **no scalar arm in `dc`** — see §3.2a |
| `sc50`, R50, SGDm+Lion, α₀=1e-6 | **2** | `additive` r=0.06 **only**. No ladder. No `shrink`. No `zpool` |

The r-optimum of 0.06 was located in a cell (ResNet-18, SGDm+Lion, α₀=1e-6) where layerwise **beats**
scalar by roughly +3 pp — i.e. in a cell with no anomaly to fix. It was then transplanted, once, at
n=2–5, into the cells that do have one. That is the design error at the centre of the whole
programme, and it is an **oversight**, not a decision: no cycle records a reason for not laddering
the anomaly cells.

### 3.2a The one shot that was taken at the AdamW anomaly cell moved it 0.5 pp

Batch `dc` does contain a plain-layerwise reference at α₀=1e-3 (`dc-awscal-a1e3-s0..s4`, n=5,
plateau5 **90.909**), so an in-batch contrast is available after all:

| batch `dc`, R18, AdamW+Adam, α₀=1e-3 | plateau5 | n |
|---|---|---|
| plain layerwise (`dc-awscal-a1e3-*`) | 90.909 | 5 |

not close the anomaly there. But it is one r value, chosen in a different cell, with no ladder
around it, so this is a weak null and not a refutation. It is the whole of the evidence that the

### 3.3 What those 4 ResNet-50 runs actually say (G3)

Batch `sc50` in full, plateau5, all arms in one batch, 100 epochs:

| α₀ | scalar (n=2) | layerwise (n=2) | additive r=0.06 (n=2) | add − layerwise | add − scalar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1e-6 | 89.510 | 88.154 | **91.511** | **+3.357** | **+2.001** |
| 1e-3 | 89.245 | 90.375 | **92.678** | **+2.303** | **+3.433** |

Individual runs: `sc50-1e6-add-s0/s1` = 91.156 / 91.866; `sc50-1e3-add-s0/s1` = 92.496 / 92.860.

Read plainly: **in the one architecture where the motivating anomaly reproduces, a single
untuned hierarchical point beats both endpoints by 2.0–3.4 pp, at both initialisations.** That is
the shape the proposal predicted. It rests on **n=2 in one batch with no r-ladder**, so it is a
lead and not a result — but a lead of that size, in exactly the cell the project was commissioned
to explain, sitting unexamined in the CSV, is the thing a supervisor is entitled to be annoyed
about. It should be the first job if anything is run at all.

Caveat, stated so it is not oversold: `additive`'s r-dial is confounded with an effective
meta-step-size division (§3.5), so the R50 gain could be "ResNet-50 wants a smaller meta-step-size"
rather than "pooling helps ResNet-50". **There is no meta-step-size ladder on ResNet-50 at all**, so
that alternative is untested. G4 and G3 should be run as one design.

### 3.5 The mechanism test fires at one α₀ and fails at the other — and nobody noticed (G4)

CORRECTIONS 21 pre-registered the kill: *"if `scalar` at ms=1e-4 reaches 92.2–92.6, the whole M0/M1
family is meta-LR tuning."* Re-derived from the `ms` and `rs` batches, plain arms, ResNet-18 /
CIFAR-10 / SGDm+Lion / 100 ep:

**α₀ = 1e-3 — the test fires.**

| meta_stepsize | scalar | layerwise | batch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1e-4 | **92.305** (n=3) | 92.960 (n=3) | `ms` |
| 1e-4 | 92.198 (n=2) | 92.824 (n=2) | `rs` |
| 1e-3 | 87.967 (n=3) | 91.259 (n=3) | `ms` |

Plain **scalar** at a tuned meta-step-size reaches 92.305, inside the pre-registered band; plain
**layerwise** reaches 92.960, above the entire `additive` r-ladder at that α₀ (best 92.403). The
hierarchical family buys nothing a meta-step-size sweep does not buy more of. Verdict stands.

**α₀ = 1e-6 — the test does not fire, and cannot, because the ladder runs the wrong way.**

| meta_stepsize | scalar | layerwise | n/arm | batch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1e-5 | 19.566 | 19.570 | 5 | `ms` |
| 3e-5 | 22.739 | 22.766 | 5 | `ms` |
| 1e-4 | 45.594 | 46.362 | 5 | `ms` |
| 3e-4 | 87.649 | 90.228 | 5 | `ms` |
| 1e-3 | 87.887 | 90.921 | 18 / 24 | **cross-batch** (9 batches; batch `ms` has no 1e-3 rung) |

The `additive` optimum in this cell is **93.286**. **No rung of the meta-step-size ladder comes
within 2.3 pp of it.** Two further defects in that ladder: it has **no rung above 1e-3** on either
`scalar` or `layerwise` at either α₀ (the only ms=3e-3 runs in the corpus are 4 rows on `nodewise`
and `resnet18_blocks` at α₀=1e-3, batch `rs`), and **batch `ms` itself contains no ms=1e-3 arm at
α₀=1e-6**, so its own top rung has to be borrowed from nine other batches. The direction that could
exonerate the operator was never sampled, and the anchor of the ladder that was sampled is
cross-batch. Since pooling *divides* the effective meta-step
(the `|2p−1|` ≈ 0.097 → 10.3× factor), the matching rung is 1e-4, which scores 46.4 — i.e. the
naive equivalence is off by 47 pp in the wrong direction. Whatever `additive` is doing at α₀=1e-6,
the corpus does not currently show it to be meta-LR tuning.

**This is a live hole, not a rhetorical one.** The cheapest fix is the two-way ladder in G4:
r ∈ {0, 0.06, 1} × ms ∈ {1e-4, 3e-4, 1e-3, 3e-3, 1e-2} × 3 seeds at α₀=1e-6, **45 runs ≈ 32 GPU-h,
one batch.** If the r-axis flattens once ms is free, the operator is a meta-LR dial and the idea is
closed properly. If it does not, the idea is not dead and §3.3 becomes urgent.

### 3.6 ImageNet — the honest position (G10)

**Never run. Not runnable here. Verified on the tree today, not quoted.**

Data, at `/cluster/account_a/imagenet_data/ILSVRC/Data/CLS-LOC`:

* **489 of 1000 train class directories**, 68 GB (of ~148 GB). The classes present are complete —
  it is a partial download, not a corrupt one.
* **50,000 validation JPEGs in one flat directory, no class subdirectories.** A search of the whole
  tree for `*devkit*`, `*ground_truth*` and `*.mat` returns **nothing**. **The validation set is
  unlabelled**, so `plateau5` cannot be computed at *any* number of classes. This blocks ImageNet
  independently of the missing 511 classes, and it is the decisive blocker.

Code, which is worse and is the sharper point:

* `imagenet/Optimizers/HF.py` line 153:
  `self.stepsize_type = stepsize_groups if stepsize_groups in ['scalar'] else 'blockwise'`
  — the ImageNet copy of the optimizer accepts **`scalar` only**; `layerwise`, `nodewise` and
  `weightwise` are not in its list and not in its docstring. It can do `scalar` and `blockwise`
  (`resnet18_blocks` = [3,12,15,15,15,2], `resnet50_blocks` = [3,30,39,57,30,2]) and nothing else.
  It carries **0** of our patches (260 lines against the CIFAR copy's 710).
* `imagenet/load_data.py` reads validation with `datasets.ImageFolder(root='ILSVRC2012/validation')`
  — which requires class subdirectories the on-disk copy does not have.

So: **the parent paper's blockwise-on-ImageNet claim has an executable path in the released code;
the layerwise-on-ImageNet claim does not.** That is worth putting to Arsalan directly. (FINDINGS
also records that the authors' own launcher on scratch, `cedar_arg_iterator_HF.sh`, sweeps
`scalar` only with `resnet18_blocks` commented out — I did not re-open that directory, which is
the PI's and off-limits without permission.)

Cost, if the data were complete and labelled. A ResNet-50 / ImageNet-1k 90-epoch run is
≈90 × 1.28M × 3 × 4.1 GFLOP ≈ **1.4 × 10¹⁸ FLOP**; an L4 at a realistic 30–50 % MFU delivers
≈10–15 TFLOP/s, so **≈30–40 GPU-h per run at best, 60–100 in practice** with this codebase's
Python-side meta overhead. A 9-rung r-ladder × 3 seeds × 2 arms is 54 runs = **3,000–5,000 GPU-h**.
That is 2–3× the *entire* corpus to date. **Infeasible on cost alone**, before the credentials
question.

**The substitute that was never considered.** The record's substitutes were CIFAR-100 and a budget
sweep — both same-resolution, so neither moves the *scale/resolution* axis the premise is about.
The credential-free option is **Tiny-ImageNet** (200 classes, 100k train images at 64×64, ~240 MB,
free download from cs231n, labelled val): ≈8× CIFAR-10 per epoch, so **≈5.6 GPU-h per 100-epoch
ResNet-18 run**, and a 9-rung r-ladder × 3 seeds plus scalar/layerwise controls ≈ 36 runs ≈
**200 GPU-h.** Downsampled ImageNet-1k at 64×64 (Chrabaszcz et al.) would be better still but needs
the same ImageNet account, and costs ≈71 GPU-h per run (≈850 GPU-h for a thin ladder).
**Tiny-ImageNet is affordable, needs no credentials, and was never proposed. That is an oversight.**

**What the absence does to the enterprise.** The proposal's premise is a claim about a regime the
project never entered. The mitigation is that the *phenomenon* — layerwise below scalar — was
reproduced three times at CIFAR cost (§3.1) without anyone noticing, so the diagnosis could have
been attempted on reproducible ground. It was not, because the fix was only ever laddered in cells
where the phenomenon is absent. That, and not the missing ImageNet, is what stopped the diagnosis.

## 6. G13–G19 and the rest — what else a supervisor will ask

**"Did you vary the batch size?"** No. `batch_size = 100` in **all 2,174 rows** of the corpus. This
is not a minor axis for this idea: batch size sets steps-per-epoch, which sets the number of meta
updates, which is the exact quantity the shrink saturation turns on. A 4× larger batch quarters the
meta-steps and multiplies every half-life-in-run by 4. **12 runs ≈ 8 GPU-h** would tell you whether
the M0 "flat ladder" is a property of the operator or of 50,000 steps. **Overlooked.**

**"Did you check it outside the one β-clip box?"** No — all 637 at `-15:-2.3026`, while the corpus
uses 13 boxes, and the anomaly's size co-varies with the box across batches (`a0`, box
`-15:-2.3026`, −1.889 pp; `tc1`, box `-30:6.0`, −1.561 pp, same nominal cell). Batch and box are
confounded in that pair, which is precisely why a within-batch box contrast is needed and does not
exist. **12 runs ≈ 8 GPU-h. Overlooked.**

**"Is the confound a property of the operator or of Lion?"** Untested. The `|2p−1|` argument works
*because* Lion's meta-increment has constant magnitude ±ms; with a non-normalised meta-optimizer
(plain SGD on β) it would not apply. Every meta in the corpus is `Lion`, `Adam`, `RMSProp` or
`fixed` — **no non-normalised meta anywhere**. This is arguably a cleaner confound-breaker than G4.
**~24 runs ≈ 17 GPU-h. Overlooked.**

**"Did you try letting the model choose the pooling strength?"** No. Empirical-Bayes / James–Stein
shrinkage — λ_t = 1 − (m−2)σ̂²/‖β_t − β̄_t‖², estimated on-line from the between-group spread — is
the textbook hierarchical answer, needs **no ladder and no tuning**, and directly answers the
obvious referee objection ("you added a hyper-parameter to a method whose selling point is having
none"). **Never proposed in any cycle. Patch ≈30 lines + 18 runs ≈ 13 GPU-h. Overlooked, and it is
the one I would be most embarrassed about.**

**"Is the hierarchy nested properly?"** No. Every operator is two-level: group vs. global. The
natural structure for a ResNet is three-level — scalar → block → layer — with a strength at each
level. Never built. **Overlooked**, though low priority until the two-level case is settled.

**"How does any of this compare to just tuning the learning rate?"** It does not, and it was never
asked. Every hierarchical contrast in the 637 runs is MetaOptimize-vs-MetaOptimize. In the same
corpus, at ResNet-18/CIFAR-10/100 epochs, plain SGD at lr=0.1 reaches **95.282 / 95.180 / 95.082 /
95.040 / 95.038** (batch `bl`, plateau5, n=5) and tuned AdamW reaches **94.962**, while the best
hierarchical run anywhere in the corpus is **94.092** (`r34r-r002-s2`, ResNet-34) and the best on
ResNet-18 is **94.040** (`bo-lion-r0-s0`). At 300/600 epochs the cosine AdamW baseline gives
**95.02–95.26** against the hierarchical arms' 93.0–93.6. **The entire hierarchical family sits
1–2 pp below a tuned fixed-hyper-parameter baseline on the same architecture and budget.** This
costs **0 GPU-h** to state and should be stated. It does not invalidate the internal comparison —
MetaOptimize's claim is about tuning-free adaptation, not about beating a tuned baseline — but
omitting it would be the kind of omission that gets found.

**"Did the budget ever erase the effect?"** Partly, and the two operators behave differently. Within
batch `e3a` (α₀=1e-6, 300 epochs, ResNet-18): plain layerwise 91.764, `shrink` λ=0.1 **91.826**
(+0.062, against +1.534 for the same λ at 100 epochs in `h2`) — **`shrink`'s advantage collapses
with budget**; `additive` r=0 92.056, r=0.05 93.016, r=0.1 **93.313** (+1.549 over plain, against
the batch-matched +2.530 for r=0.06 at 100 epochs in batch `sc`, where plain layerwise is 90.772
and additive r=0.06 is 93.302) — **`additive`'s does not.** That divergence is unexplained and
rests on n=2 in one batch with no scalar arm. **G17.**

**"Did you look at the trajectories, not just the endpoint?"** Partly — `PATCH_PROBE` records
per-step β and meta-gradient statistics and `probe.jsonl` files exist for the `zb`/`zsw` batches.
But **no probe was ever collected on the ResNet-50 anomaly cell, on the AdamW anomaly cell, or on
any `shrink` λ ladder rung**, so the one thing that would say *why* the interior optimum exists at
α₀=1e-6 — the β trajectory's spread over training — was never measured where it matters. Probes are
nearly free (they run inside the training job). **Overlooked.**

**"Why is `I1` +0.136 and `a0` −1.889 in the same nominal cell?"** Unresolved. Same network, same
optimiser pair, same α₀, same β-clip box, same augmentation, n=3 each. Whatever distinguishes them
is not in the CSV's columns, which under RULE 20 means somebody has to read the two batches' own
`ARGS` lines. **I did not do this** — the `.out` files are not in this repo (they live at `../runs`
and `../runs_cluster_b`) and this task did not extend to opening them. **UNSURE, and worth one hour.**
