**Every number below was re-derived from the CSV and the raw `.out` epoch series at this HEAD**,

**Cell convention.** A cell is matched on 13 axes: `network, dataset, batch_size, granularity,

## 1. What the idea was, and what happened to it

Arsalan Sharifnassab's November-2025 proposal started from an anomaly in the parent paper
(Sharifnassab, Salehkaleybar & Sutton, ICML 2025): in MetaOptimize, layerwise/blockwise step sizes
beat a scalar step size on small networks but lose to it on ImageNet, even though the layerwise
parameterisation strictly contains the scalar one. The proposed fix was hierarchical shrinkage —
learn a scalar step size and layerwise step sizes jointly and pull the layerwise ones toward the
scalar with a tunable strength — and the intended contribution was the *diagnosis* of why the more
expressive parameterisation loses. **That idea is dead, and it died in an awkward way: the version
that was proposed was never measured, and the two operators that were measured for 550 runs turned
out to be adjusting the meta-learning rate rather than the pooling.** 637 runs and 492.8 GPU-hours
— 29.3% of the corpus's runs and 30.0% of its hours — went into four operators. The empirical curve
they produced is real: on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 at `alpha0=1e-6` there is an interior optimum at a
retention ratio r ≈ 0.05–0.07 worth **+0.997 pp on plateau5** over the better endpoint (93.285 ±
0.119, n=8, versus 92.288 ± 0.117, n=6), and it reproduces on ResNet-10 and ResNet-34. But on a
fully axis-matched cell at a proper initialisation, a plain **scalar** step size with the
meta-stepsize turned down one decade matches the best hierarchical arm to +0.140 pp (se 0.112,
t=1.25, n=7 vs 5) and a plain **layerwise** arm with the same tune beats it by 0.484 pp (se 0.074,
t=−6.53, n=7 vs 11); under the one operator that pools without that confound, full pooling *costs*
3.013 pp (se 0.084, t=−35.90); and at its own best point the whole MetaOptimize-plus-hierarchy
stack loses to AdamW with a cosine schedule by 0.86–2.37 pp and to plain SGD at lr=0.1 by about
1.8 pp. Meanwhile the shrinkage coefficient the proposal actually named has exactly one live
decade, `[1e-4, 1e-3]`, and **zero runs sit inside it** — the ten-rung ladder that was run is
statistically flat above 1e-3 (ANOVA F(6,20)=2.012, p=0.112, N=27) because the operator applies its
contraction on every optimizer step and saturates within 693 steps of the 50,000 in a run. The
honest one-line summary is not "hierarchical shrinkage fails"; it is **"hierarchical shrinkage as
proposed was never tested, and everything that was tested instead measured something else."**

## 3. The three findings that killed it

### 3.1 The additive r-dial is a meta-learning-rate dial, not a pooling dial

**Decisive numbers.** On a cell matched on twelve axes (ResNet-18 / CIFAR-10 / layerwise / SGDm +
Lion / augment=1 / clip −15:−2.3026 / batch 100 / γ=1 / 100 epochs / `alpha0=1e-3`), differing only
in `hier` and `meta_stepsize`, all on plateau5:

| arm | plateau5 | n |
|---|---|---|
| plain scalar, ms=1e-3 | 87.839 ± 0.240 | 14 |
| plain layerwise, ms=1e-3 | 91.196 ± 0.193 | 14 |
| additive r=0 (nominal "full pooling"), ms=1e-3 | 92.217 ± 0.075 | 7 |
| **additive r=0.1 (best interior), ms=1e-3** | **92.403 ± 0.139** | **7** |
| **plain scalar, ms=1e-4** | **92.262 ± 0.222** | **5** |
| **plain layerwise, ms=1e-4** | **92.887 ± 0.173** | **11** |

`additive r=0.1 − tuned scalar = +0.140, se 0.112, t=1.25` (not significant).
`additive r=0.1 − tuned layerwise = −0.484, se 0.074, t=−6.53`.

**The mechanism.** Under a Lion meta-optimizer every component of the realised β-update is exactly
±ms. `patches/HF_patched.py::_apply_hier` pools the *realised update* — `β ← β_prev + d̄ + r·(d − d̄)`
— so at r=0 every group takes the mean of m sign-valued increments, whose magnitude is
ms·|2p−1| where p is the fraction positive. Pooling therefore divides the effective meta-stepsize
by |2p−1|. Measured (`results/p7_c36.txt`, "CORRECTED LADDER", STEADY window, R18/CIFAR-10, n=10):
|2p−1| = **0.3947** at m=6 (blk6) / **0.1204** at m=62 (layerwise) / **0.01465** at m=14,420
(nodewise) / **3.354e-4** at m=11.17M (weightwise). At layerwise that is a **8.3×** reduction; the
record carries 7.8× (from the earlier uncorrected `p7_c31.txt` pass, |2p−1|=0.1277) and 10.3×
(from a `frac_neg`=0.5484 estimate, `docs/CORRECTIONS.md:619`) — three scales for the same quantity,
all in the 8–10× band. `p7_c36.txt` also records that 83.8% of the layerwise |2p−1| is the
binomial null floor; that does not weaken the mechanism, it *is* the mechanism — averaging m
sign-valued increments shrinks their magnitude whether or not the signs carry signal.

**Two corroborations.** (i) A plain scalar arm run at exactly the ms the mechanism predicts
(1.277e-4, batch `msa`) reads **91.849 ± 0.131, n=5** — between the two ladder rungs, as it should
be. (ii) The interior optimum exists only where |2p−1| is intermediate: it is worth +2.314 pp at
layerwise and +1.459 at nodewise, +0.468 (inside the floor) at blk6 where there is almost no
reduction available, and does not exist at all at weightwise where β cannot move (row 17).

**The honest caveat, which the record does not make.** This test **fires at `alpha0=1e-3` and
cannot fire at `alpha0=1e-6`.** At a0=1e-6 the additive optimum is 93.285 and *no rung of the
meta-stepsize ladder comes within 2.3 pp of it* — scalar 1e-5 19.566 / 3e-5 22.739 / 1e-4 45.594 /
3e-4 87.649 / 1e-3 87.879 (n=5 each, n=12 at 1e-3), and the ladder has no rung above 1e-3 at that
a0. So at a0=1e-6 the +2.4 pp is *not* explained by an effective-meta-stepsize reduction and
remains unexplained. The argument that a0=1e-6 does not matter rests on row 12 — that a proper init
removes 81% of the interior optimum — not on the mechanism test. **The r × ms two-way ladder that
would settle this at a0=1e-6 was never run** (gap G4).

**Re-derive:** `analysis/` conventions; select the twelve axes above and group by `hier`,
`eta_ratio`, `meta_stepsize`; collapse `dup_group`; Welch on plateau5. Probe values:
`sed -n '220,232p' results/p7_c36.txt`.

### 3.2 Under the operator that actually pools, pooling loses

`zpool` operates on the meta-gradient before `meta_update`, so its endpoints are algebraic
identities: r=1 is plain exactly, r=0 is scalar exactly (`patches/HF_patched.py::_zpool`). Both
verify empirically (row 22): layerwise r=1 = 91.037 ± 0.176 (n=5) against plain layerwise 90.893 ±
0.191 (n=17); r=0 = 87.880 against plain scalar 87.879 (n=12).

**Decisive numbers, all plateau5, R18/CIFAR-10, a0=1e-6, 100 ep:**

* Full true pooling costs **−3.013 pp** against the matched plain layerwise arm (se 0.084,
  t=−35.90, n=5 vs 17); **−3.157 pp** against the operator's own r=1 arm (n=5 vs 5).
* There is **no interior optimum at a good partition**: the nine-rung layerwise ladder peaks at
  r=0.99 = 91.112 (**n=1**), +0.075 pp over r=1, inside the ±0.176 seed band.
* Pooling **does** rescue an over-fine partition: weightwise r=0.99 = 89.272 ± 0.229 (n=2) against
  r=1 = 79.235 ± 0.345 (n=5), **+10.037 pp** (se 0.224, t=44.85). But that best true-pooling number
  anywhere is **2.176 pp below plain blk6** (91.448, n=12) and 1.621 below plain layerwise.
* True pooling is granularity-invariant at r=0 (span **0.063 pp** across 1.9×10⁶ in m), which is
  what a pooling operator must be. Additive r=0 spans **44.404 pp** over the same range. The
  claim carried in the handoff for ~20 cycles that "even full pooling helps, +1.4 pp" read the
  additive r=0 cell and is void.

The defensible sentence is: **pooling rescues an over-fine partition and never improves a good one.
You do not reach a good partition by pooling a bad one; you reach it by choosing a good partition.**
Caveat the record already makes and I confirm from the code: only the *endpoints* of the zpool
ladder are clean — at r=0 the shared term is the **sum**, not the mean, so interior cells train at
3–35× smaller effective step. `zmpool` was written to fix exactly that (it uses the mean) and got
four 20-epoch runs.

**Re-derive:** filter `hier=zpool`, the a0=1e-6 100-epoch home axes, group by `granularity` and
`eta_ratio`.

## 5. What the effort produced instead

The corpus turned into a different paper: a count-matched audit of step-size *partition* structure,
which asks whether the group **partition** matters once the group **count** is held fixed —
the confound the parent paper's granularity result carries. The uniform partition wins **all twenty
count-matched cells** on ResNet-18, ResNet-34 and ResNet-50, pooling to **+0.556 ± 0.045 pp**
(calibrated 95% CI ± 0.121) over eight SGDm cells and homogeneous against that null (Q 4.21, median
9.4); alignment at fixed count and size multiset is a bounded null (−0.009 ± 0.157 pp, replicated
at −0.018 ± 0.079); and of **nine candidate mechanisms examined, none is a general carrier.** It is
at desk-accept for TMLR (9/10, `structural_gaps = []`, 0 blocking), the audit script
`analysis/c98_reproduce.py` exits 0 with all 636 checks passing, and the paper states its own
competitiveness deficit — the method trails tuned baselines by 1.8–4.2 pp, dwarfing the ≈0.6 pp
effect it measures.

## 6. What is still open, if the idea is to be pursued

In the order I would run them.

1. **G1 — build the operator the proposal describes.** A prox/penalty form (λ‖β − β̄‖² added into
   the meta-gradient) or the two-level β_g = β_scalar + δ_g. λ=0 must be exactly plain; verify that
   as an equivalence test before any GPU. ~42–46 GPU-h. **Without this the idea has no measurement
   behind it and cannot be called refuted.**
2. **G4 — the r × meta-stepsize ladder at a0=1e-6.** 45 runs, ~32 GPU-h. This is the only
   experiment that can retire the mechanism question at the initialisation where the effect is
   sharpest, and it is the cheapest thing on the list.
3. **G3 — ladder the ResNet-50 cell.** ~137 GPU-h. The anomaly is there, one untuned point already
   beats both endpoints by 2.0–3.4 pp at both a0, and nobody followed it up.
4. **G16 — the empirical-Bayes λ.** ~13 GPU-h. Removes the hyper-parameter objection entirely.
5. **G8 + G7 — zpool on a partition that works** (`resnet18_blocks`, and the count-matched
   `chunk*` family). ~25–63 GPU-h. Turns "pooling never improves a good partition" from an
   extrapolation into a measurement, and separates pooling from coarsening.
6. **G15 — one arm with a non-normalised meta-optimizer.** ~17 GPU-h. Removes the |2p−1| mechanism
   rather than measuring around it.
7. **G18 + G20 — free.** State the tuned-baseline comparison (the runs exist); attach `PROBE` to
   whatever is run above.

A defensible minimum programme is items 1, 2 and 4: **~90 GPU-h**, against the 1,641.5 already
spent, and it would let the idea be closed on evidence rather than on an implementation artefact.

## 8. Marked UNSURE — things I could not settle

1. **Why `a0` and `I1` differ by 1.87 pp on the layerwise arm under identical ARGS lines.** Ruled
   out: every training flag, the CSV's 13 axes, seed. Not ruled out: GPU model, driver/library
   version, node, or a genuinely bimodal layerwise trajectory at a0=1e-3. `wallclock_min` and
   `node` differ between the batches but that is expected. **This is the single most important
   loose end in the premise**, and it is an hours-not-GPU investigation.
2. **The record's M0 reference cell** ("plain layerwise 91.33 ± 0.17, n=6", `best_test`) cannot be
   reconstructed from the CSV under any single-family, clip or augment filter I tried. The
   13-axis-matched cell is n=17 (`best_test` 91.200 ± 0.179 / plateau5 90.893 ± 0.191).
3. **Reconciling the three |2p−1| scales** (8.3× from the corrected `p7_c36` STEADY ladder, 7.8×
   from `p7_c31`, 10.3× from `frac_neg` in CORRECTIONS 619). They differ by measurement window and
   by pass, not by definition, and all lie in the 8–10× band; I did not chase which is canonical.
4. **Whether the a0=1e-6 additive gain has any explanation at all.** The meta-stepsize account
